Newsletter costs at 1,000 and 5,000 subscribers

Verified official prices, monthly versus annual billing, and a worked paid-subscription example.

Reviewed November 8, 2026

List size matters, but so do required features and how you charge readers. A free newsletter and a paid publication have different cost structures.

Substack: free publishing, revenue-based fees

According to Substack, publishing has no fixed fee regardless of list size. Enabling paid subscriptions adds a 10% platform share of that revenue. Payment processing is an additional expense that varies by circumstances.

Two different scenarios

1,000 free readers: platform cost can be zero on beehiiv Free or Substack. A premium plan needs a feature-based justification; paying for software does not create an audience.

5,000 readers, 200 paying $5/month: hypothetical subscription revenue is $1,000/month. Substack’s platform fee would be $100/month; Lite would cost $95/month equivalent with a $1,140 annual payment, or $109 on monthly billing. Billing cadence changes the comparison. This excludes payment processing, taxes, churn and other expenses.

Build a complete budget

  • Platform subscription and revenue share.
  • Payment processing, refunds and currency conversion.
  • Domain, design, contributors and editing time.
  • Reader acquisition and other recurring costs.

Use one currency throughout a scenario. Annual billing may lower the monthly equivalent but requires an upfront payment. Adjust periods before comparing a monthly bill against an annual total.
