At what revenue do fees match?

A $49 monthly-equivalent fixed fee matches a 10% commission at $490 monthly revenue: 49 ÷ 0.10. This example uses Lite for 1,000 contacts, billed $588 annually. Below the threshold, 10% costs less; above it, the fixed fee costs less provided that the same audience tier remains applicable.

This is not an automatic migration recommendation. Total audience growth can change the plan quote, and payment fees may differ. Our free comparison calculator lets you edit each assumption.

When would I investigate each option?

Substack: when avoiding a fixed publishing subscription matters and you accept that platform fees grow with paid revenue. Evaluate the editor, reader experience and your distribution needs.

beehiiv: when you need paid-plan features and can justify recurring expenditure. Check automations, monetization, limits and eligibility independently; anticipated earnings alone do not justify purchasing.

Managed versus self-hosted: include hidden costs

Comparing subscriptions alone can mislead: self-hosted newsletters may require server hosting, delivery APIs, backups, security updates and ongoing maintenance. Use our free total-cost worksheet to compare managed platforms with self-hosting before deciding.

A four-step decision check

  1. Separate total readers from paying subscribers.
  2. Model three revenue scenarios using the actual audience-tier quote.
  3. Test publishing and review unsubscribe and export workflows.
  4. Estimate migration effort and the time a switch would save.

Common questions

Does a smaller platform fee mean more profit?

Not necessarily. Work, acquisition, processing and retention still matter. Our calculator compares platform costs, not final profit.

Should I migrate as soon as the fees match?

No. Consider how long savings would take to repay the migration effort and which features you would gain or lose.
